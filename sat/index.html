A real question from the bank

Many economists have predicted that raising the minimum wage will reduce employment, since employers confronting higher labor costs will hire fewer workers or eliminate existing positions. When New Jersey raised its minimum wage in 1992, economists David Card and Alan Krueger compared fast-food restaurants there with similar restaurants in neighboring Pennsylvania, where the minimum wage was unchanged, and found no indication that employment declined in New Jersey, although meal prices rose there relative to Pennsylvania. Because those restaurants apparently passed much of the added cost on to their customers, the study suggests that ______

Which choice most logically completes the text?

Step-by-step solution

  1. The prediction: higher wages mean fewer jobs.
  2. The finding: jobs held steady in New Jersey while meal prices rose.
  3. So the effect may depend on passing costs on to customers: choice B.

Why B is correct

Correct. Employment held steady while prices rose, so passing costs on may be what let employers keep their workers.

Why each other choice fails

  • ATrap: Ignores a qualificationOne study of one industry in one state cannot show that wage increases “never” reduce employment anywhere.
  • CTrap: Goes beyond the textThe text reports employment and prices, not profits, so it gives no basis for comparing profitability.
  • DTrap: Goes beyond the textThe text says nothing about customers’ preferences regarding the wages that restaurants paid.

The underlying concept

A logical completion is the conclusion that the text’s own evidence supports: one careful step beyond it and no further.

Fastest reliable method

Restate the evidence in your own words, predict the conclusion before reading the choices, then pick the choice that matches your prediction.

Common trap

A choice that overstates the evidence, reverses it, or brings in a claim the text never supports.

What to remember

The answer follows from the text, and it is usually the most cautious reasonable conclusion.
