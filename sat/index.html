A real question from the bank

Some economists argue that raising a city’s minimum wage will inevitably reduce employment in restaurants, since owners facing higher labor costs will hire fewer workers or eliminate existing positions. Yet when the city of Port Avery raised its minimum wage, restaurant employment held steady while menu prices rose by approximately 3 percent. Since restaurant owners there apparently covered their higher labor costs through pricing, the Port Avery case suggests that ______

Which choice most logically completes the text?

Step-by-step solution

  1. The claim being questioned says job losses are inevitable. In Port Avery, jobs held steady because owners raised prices to cover costs.
  2. One case cannot prove a universal rule, but it can show that the outcome depends on a condition: here, the ability to raise prices.
  3. Choice B draws exactly that limited conclusion.

Why B is correct

Correct. It ties the employment outcome to the mechanism the text identifies (covering costs through pricing) and is cautiously worded.

Why each other choice fails

  • ATrap: Ignores a qualification“Never” is as sweeping as the “inevitably” the text questions. One city’s experience cannot support a universal claim.
  • CTrap: Goes beyond the textThe text says nothing about profits or about restaurants elsewhere.
  • DTrap: Goes beyond the textCustomers kept buying despite higher prices, but the text gives no information about their preferences regarding wages.

The underlying concept

A single counterexample can disprove “always” but cannot prove “never.” It supports “it depends.”

Fastest reliable method

Use the clause right before the blank (“covered their higher labor costs through pricing”) as the core of your prediction.

Common trap

Replacing one absolute claim with the opposite absolute claim.

What to remember

One case → “may,” “can,” “depends.” Not “never” or “always.”
